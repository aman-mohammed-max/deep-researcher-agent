Evaluate the engineering viability and physical constraints of utilizing High-Temperature Superconductors (HTS), specifically Rare-Earth Barium Copper Oxide (REBCO) coated conductors, in commercial magnetically confined nuclear fusion reactors. Specifically investigate: (1) REBCO tape critical current density (J_c) and upper critical field performance under operating temperatures of 20 K versus liquid helium 4.2 K; (2) Mechanical stress limits, Lorentz forces, and structural reinforcement required for 20+ Tesla magnetic fields in central solenoids and toroidal field coils; (3) Cryogenic cooling power scaling and heat deposition removal under continuous 14.1 MeV D-T neutron irradiation; (4) Quench detection challenges, localized hotspot propagation, and tape delamination failure modes in compact tokamak designs such as CFS SPARC and Tokamak Energy; and (5) Economic comparison and power balance scaling (Q > 10) between compact high-field HTS tokamaks and conventional low-field copper/LTS architectures like ITER. Provide exact physical parameters, material limits, and engineering trade-offs.
